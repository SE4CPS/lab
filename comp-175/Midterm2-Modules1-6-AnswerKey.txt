PART 2: DIAGRAM QUESTION (19) -- GRADING GUIDE
================================================================

Full credit needs:
- Kali VM and Ubuntu VM shown as distinct boxes
- Their host/hypervisor shown as a distinct box underneath both
- The connection between Kali and Ubuntu labeled SCP (over SSH)
- The log file's location shown on the Ubuntu side before transfer

Partial credit: diagram shows the two VMs and the connection but
omits the host/hypervisor box, or doesn't label the connection.

No credit: diagram shows only one machine, or doesn't involve SSH
at all (e.g. shows a USB drive or a generic "network" cloud).

PART 3: WRITTEN RECOMMENDATION (20) -- GRADING GUIDE
================================================================

Strong answer identifies:
- Standard owner/group permission bits as the base: put all service
  writers in one group with rw on the directory, everyone else gets
  no bits (e.g. 770, group = service-writers).
- An ACL entry to grant the parts-counter group read-only access
  without adding them to the service-writers group (e.g.
  setfacl -m g:parts-counter:r-- on the directory).
- For the audit-trail follow-up: inotify-based monitoring (or, where
  available, a real audit daemon) watching the directory so that any
  access -- including a sysadmin's -- is logged outside of DAC, since
  DAC alone can't stop an administrator from simply re-granting
  themselves access.

Partial credit: gets the permission/ACL split right but misses the
audit-trail follow-up, or proposes a workable but less precise
mechanism (e.g. a single shared group for everyone, explained
correctly as a trade-off).

No credit: recommends world-readable/writable access, or doesn't
distinguish the three access levels requested in the scenario.
